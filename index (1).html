Curiosity, First

01 / About

Before biology was a career, it was dinosaurs, the kid at the dinner table talking everyone's ear off about the Mesozoic, rebuilding whole stories from a handful of fragments. In high school, the Ti plasmid, proof that nature had already found a way to correct and rewrite a genome long before scientists did, the way Agrobacterium tumefaciens naturally edits a plant cell's DNA, turned that curiosity toward biotechnology for good.

Research

I work as a Senior Researcher in the Vasudev Lab at UMass Dartmouth, where one question keeps me at the bench: could the exosomes SKOV3 ovarian cancer cells shed into the body be quietly rewriting the odds for breast cancer too? That curiosity is why I lead a side project designing de novo antibodies for hemophilia A patients whose bodies have turned against their own treatment, and why my favorite piece of coursework wasn't really an assignment, it was chasing down why a coral-reef bacterium, Serratia marcescens, decides to switch sides under stress. Even my capstone at Sathyabama Institute of Science and Technology, coaxing a nanoparticle into flipping on a growth pathway in radish seeds, circled back to the same idea: find the switch, then understand what flips it.

Industry

Industry is where that instinct met scale. At Fujifilm Biosciences, watching inclusion bodies form during fermentation, then working out how to refold them into something functional, is what convinced me protein folding isn't abstract theory, it's the difference between a drug that works and one that doesn't. Four years earlier at Zifo Scientific Informatics, I embedded assay logic, screens, and dose-response data straight into the Electronic Lab Notebooks scientists opened every morning, and saw firsthand why labs still running on paper batch records can't keep pace with Pharma 4.0: without that data captured digitally and traceably from the start, none of the automation or analytics built on top of it has anything to stand on.

Sustainable science, the kind that leaves things better positioned instead of just proving a point, is the standard I hold my own work to.

02 / Active Projects
SENIOR RESEARCHER · SINCE MAR 2025

Extracellular Vesicles from SKOV3 Ovarian Cancer Cells

In plain terms

Cells normally shed extracellular vesicles, or exosomes, to clear out cellular waste. Cancer cells hijack that same mechanism, packaging vesicles with proteins and genetic material that may help a tumor spread. Studying what's packed inside could one day reveal what a tumor is doing without a biopsy.

  • Characterizing EVs secreted by SKOV3 ovarian cancer cells grown in a suspension bioreactor system
  • Using SEC, dynamic light scattering, and scanning transmission electron microscopy to profile biophysical and molecular properties
  • Profiling surface marker expression and miRNA cargo
  • Investigating potential as non-invasive biomarkers of cancer progression

PROJECT LEAD · SINCE MAR 2026

De Novo Anti-Idiotypic Antibody Design in Hemophilia A

In plain terms

Some hemophilia A patients' bodies learn to attack the treatment meant to help them. An anti-idiotypic antibody binds those attacking antibodies, calming the immune response so replacement therapy can keep working.

  • Part of a collaborative AI/bioinformatics team exploring de novo anti-idiotypic antibody design
  • Target: antibodies some hemophilia A patients develop against replacement Factor VIII therapy
  • Currently in literature analysis on patient-derived anti-Factor VIII antibodies, and brainstorming design possibilities at the intersection of AI and protein design
  • Next step: applying RFdiffusion and AlphaFold2 to candidate designs
